# Mode A — Build from Scratch

Load this when the founder is building a value proposition from scratch. Walk the three phases
in order; the founder's product stays out until Synthesis. As each phase resolves, have them
write that line into the matching section of their template.

## Phase 1 — The Problem

Ask: "Phase 1 of 3 — what problem does your customer have? Describe it in their words, not yours. Keep your product out of it for now."

If discovery data has a clear problem signal, anchor it: "Your data suggests [strongest signal] is the recurring pain — is that the core problem, or is something else sharper?"

| Answer type | Response |
|---|---|
| Customer-language problem ("they spend hours doing X manually") | Confirm, have them write it into the Problem section, move to Phase 2 |
| Solution description ("my product helps them...") | "Hold the product for now — what's the problem they have before your product exists?" |
| Multiple problems | "Which one is most painful for them?" |
| Investor framing ("the market lacks...") | "How would the customer describe this problem to a friend?" |

Move on when: they have a specific, customer-language problem written into the Problem section.

## Phase 2 — For Whom

Ask: "Phase 2 of 3 — who specifically has this problem? Not a segment — a person. What's their situation?"

If discovery data names a clear customer type, anchor it: "You talked to [customer type from data] — is that the person, or is the problem sharpest for someone else?"

| Answer type | Response |
|---|---|
| Specific person with context ("a solo founder who just hit 10 customers") | Confirm, have them write the Customer section, move to Phase 3 |
| Demographic segment ("small business owners") | "Can you name one real person from your interviews who has this problem?" |
| Multiple customers | "Which one are you solving it for first?" |
| Vague role ("busy professionals") | "What are they doing when this problem hits them?" |

Move on when: they have a specific person or situation, not a category.

## Phase 3 — The Alternative

Ask: "Phase 3 of 3 — what do they do today instead? And what does that cost them — time, money, frustration?"

If discovery data named workarounds, anchor it: "Your data shows [workaround from data] — is that right, and what does it cost them?"

| Answer type | Response |
|---|---|
| Workaround + cost ("they use spreadsheets, takes 3 hours each week") | Confirm, have them write the Alternative + Cost section, move to Synthesis |
| "They do nothing" | "Why do they live with the problem? What's the consequence of not solving it?" |
| Competitor named | "What would they do if your product and that competitor both disappeared?" |
| Workaround without cost | "What does that workaround actually cost them?" |

Move on when: they have a workaround and a concrete cost.

## Synthesis

Reflect all three pieces back:

> "Here's what we have: [customer] dealing with [problem], currently [workaround], costing them [cost]. Now — what does your product do differently? One phrase is enough."

After they answer, ask: "And what's the alternative you're replacing — what would they go back to if your product disappeared?"

Then: "Now write the positioning line into your template: [Product] helps [person] who [problem], by [solution], unlike [alternative]. Take a shot."

Run the validation gate after they write it.

## Validation gate (Mode A)

Before you summarize, check all three:

| Criterion | Pass | Fail → one probe |
|---|---|---|
| No product in problem clause | Pain described without solution | "The sentence mentions your product — describe the pain without it." |
| Specific customer | A person or situation, not a segment | "Who specifically? A segment isn't enough." |
| Real consequence | Concrete cost: time, money, frustration | "What does it actually cost them?" |

Ask one probe at a time. Wait for the answer. Move to the next failed criterion. Work through all failures. Don't loop back — one pass only. When the sentence holds up, go to Closing in the SKILL.
