# Five Dimensions — Question Scripts and Response Tables

Load this during the five-dimensions section. Walk each dimension in order. One question at
a time. Don't move to the next dimension until you have a clear answer, or have flagged an
assumption and the founder has acknowledged it.

---

## Dimension 1 — Pain intensity

Say in plain text: "Looking across all your jobs: which one has the customer who is hurting
the most right now — not in six months, today?"

Then **use the AskUserQuestion tool**:
- Question text: "Which job has the customer in the most pain right now?"
- Header: "Pain intensity"
- One option per job — Label: "Job [N]", Description: [job statement]

After they select a job, follow up in plain text: "What's your evidence for that — something
you heard, observed, or can point to?"

| Answer type | Response |
|---|---|
| Names a job + cites specific evidence (quote, pattern, observation) | Accept — note the job and evidence, move to Dimension 2 |
| Names a job but no evidence | "What makes you say that — did you hear this directly, or is that an assumption? What's the most concrete signal you have?" |
| "All of them hurt equally" | "If you had to ship one thing this week for the customer in the most pain, which job would it be? Force the call." |
| Talks about market size instead of customer pain | "That's the market opportunity. I'm asking about the customer — which one is in the most pain right now, based on what you know?" |

## Dimension 2 — Quality of existing alternatives

Say in plain text: "Which job has the most broken workaround today — meaning the customer's
current way of getting it done is the most painful, slow, or unreliable? That's where the
largest opportunity gap is."

Then **use the AskUserQuestion tool**:
- Question text: "Which job has the most broken workaround?"
- Header: "Existing alternatives"
- One option per job — Label: "Job [N]", Description: [job statement]

After they select a job, follow up in plain text: "What exactly does the customer do today
to get this done? Walk me through their current process."

| Answer type | Response |
|---|---|
| Names a job + describes the specific workaround and its cost | Accept — note it, move to Dimension 3 |
| Names a job but workaround is vague | "What exactly does the customer do today to get this done? Walk me through their current process." |
| "There's no existing solution" | "If there's no solution, what do they do — nothing? Something manual? What's the downstream cost of not solving it?" |
| Names a competitor instead of a workaround | "What does the customer do if that competitor didn't exist? That's the real baseline." |

## Dimension 3 — Strategic fit and sequencing

Say in plain text: "Which job, if you solved it first, creates the best foundation for
everything that follows — in terms of what you learn, who you can reach, or what you can
build next?"

Then **use the AskUserQuestion tool**:
- Question text: "Which job unlocks the most if you solve it first?"
- Header: "Strategic fit"
- One option per job — Label: "Job [N]", Description: [job statement]

After they select a job, follow up in plain text: "What does solving this one first open
up — what would you know, or be able to do, that you couldn't from the other path?"

| Answer type | Response |
|---|---|
| Names a job with clear reasoning about what it unlocks | Accept — note it, move to Dimension 4 |
| "They all lead to the same place" | "What's different about solving Job 1 first versus Job 3 first — what would you know, or be able to do, that you couldn't from the other path?" |
| Focused on revenue rather than learning | "Revenue is one signal. What does this job teach you about your customer that the others don't? What door does it open?" |
| Focused on what's easiest to build | "Ease of build is a resourcing question, not a strategy question. If resources were equal, which job unlocks the most for what comes next?" |

---

## Dimension 4 — Founder edge

Say in plain text: "Where do you have an asymmetric advantage that a well-funded competitor
wouldn't? Think about relationships, domain trust, proprietary data, or direct access to
the customer."

Then **use the AskUserQuestion tool**:
- Question text: "Which job plays most to your asymmetric advantage?"
- Header: "Founder edge"
- One option per job — Label: "Job [N]", Description: [job statement]

After they select a job, follow up in plain text: "What specific access, relationship, or
knowledge do you have there that a competitor couldn't easily replicate?"

| Answer type | Response |
|---|---|
| Names a job + names a specific edge (a relationship, access, or trust others don't have) | Accept — note it, move to Dimension 5 |
| "I don't think I have an edge" | "You chose this problem for a reason. What do you know about this customer or this domain that someone starting fresh wouldn't? Even a small edge counts." |
| Edge described is generic ("I'm passionate", "I work fast") | "That's a trait, not an edge. What specific access, relationships, or knowledge do you have that a competitor couldn't easily replicate?" |
| Same edge applies to all jobs | "If it applies equally to all, set it aside. Is there one job where your access or relationships give you a meaningful head start over anyone else?" |

---

## Dimension 5 — Evidence quality

Say in plain text: "Last question: across all your jobs, which one has the most real evidence
behind it — things you've heard directly from customers, patterns you've seen more than once,
or signals you can point to?"

Then **use the AskUserQuestion tool**:
- Question text: "Which job has the most evidence behind it?"
- Header: "Evidence quality"
- One option per job — Label: "Job [N]", Description: [job statement]

After they select a job, follow up in plain text: "How many customers have told you this
directly? What's the most concrete signal you have?"

| Answer type | Response |
|---|---|
| Names a job + cites specific evidence (N conversations, a specific quote, a pattern) | Accept — move to Synthesis |
| Names a job but evidence is thin | "How many customers have told you this directly? What's the most concrete thing you've heard?" |
| "I haven't talked to enough customers yet" | "That's honest — and it matters. Which job has the most evidence you do have, even if it's incomplete? The ranking should reflect what you actually know." |
| Evidence is the same across all jobs | "What's the one piece of evidence you'd feel most comfortable presenting to an investor? Which job does that come from?" |
