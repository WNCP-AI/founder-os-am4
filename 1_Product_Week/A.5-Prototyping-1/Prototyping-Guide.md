# Prototyping Guide — Day 1

You built your PRD earlier today. This guide takes it the rest of the way: spec your build, then walk a single development cycle — front end, to a working web app, to real data, to production-grade code — and stop the moment your prototype can answer your hypothesis.

The work here sits in the middle of a three-stage arc: **vibe coding** (prompting an AI to generate something quickly) → **AI rapid prototyping** (structured, testable, shareable) → **engineering-grade** (production code, real security, real infrastructure). Today you are in the middle stage. Fast, structured, testable — not yet production. Knowing that sets the right expectations for what you build and what comes later.

**Two guardrails apply to every prototype, regardless of lane:**

- **No permanently-stored sensitive PII.** A prototype is for testing an idea, not holding real personal data.
- **No real payments.** Mock the checkout. Never wire a live card.

State both to your AI builder at the start of every session. Do not skip this step.

## Part 1 — Spec your build

A spec doc is a short, plain-language file that tells your AI builder exactly what to make. The rule is simple: **if a decision is not in your spec, the AI invents it** — and you rarely like what it invents. Six docs cover everything, and you do not need all six to start.

**Minimum viable spec: `PRD.MD` + `TECH_STACK.MD`.** Those two are enough to open a build tool and run your first prompt. The other four add precision exactly where your build gets visual, data-driven, or complex — and the cycle in Part 2 tells you which stage needs which doc.

| Doc | Status | Used in | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| `PRD.MD` | Required | Every stage | The single source of truth for what you are building and why. |
| `TECH_STACK.MD` | Required | Every stage | Exactly which tools, services, and structure to use. |
| `UI.MD` | Recommended | Stage 01 | Your screens and how a user moves through them. |
| `DESIGN.MD` | Tool-generated | Stage 01 | Your visual language — generated by Claude Design / Stitch. |
| `DATA.MD` | Optional | Stage 03 | Your tables, fields, and access rules. |
| `API.MD` | Optional | Stage 04 | The endpoints your backend exposes. |

### PRD.MD — Product Requirements Document

The single source of truth for what you are building and why. Every AI builder reads this first. You already wrote it this morning — this is the doc the whole cycle runs on. The most important line is the hypothesis sentence in Section 5; if it is weak, everything downstream is too.

- **Status:** Required — every stage.
- **Template:** [templates/PRD.MD](templates/PRD.MD)
- **How:** open the template, find "Ask the LLM," and paste prompts 1–6 into Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini one at a time.

### TECH_STACK.MD — Technology Stack

Tells the builder exactly which tools, services, and structure to use. Skip it and you may get a random framework on the first attempt and lose an hour undoing it. It also names where you stop on the cycle, which is why it pins your whole build down.

- **Status:** Required — every stage.
- **Template:** [templates/TECH_STACK.MD](templates/TECH_STACK.MD)
- **How:** use the "Which lane am I in" table to get your default stack, then paste prompts 1–2 to confirm and fill gaps.

### UI.MD — Screen Layout & Flow

Describes your screens and how a user moves through them. Founder-written — you know your user, the AI does not. Pays off the moment you have more than two or three screens, and feeds straight into the front-end stage of the cycle.

- **Status:** Recommended — Stage 01 (front end).
- **Template:** [templates/UI.MD](templates/UI.MD)
- **How:** start from your PRD's one flow (Section 5), then paste prompts 1–2 to turn it into a screen inventory and rough wireframes.

### DESIGN.MD — Design System

Your product's visual language — colors, type, spacing, components. You do not write this from scratch. [Claude Design](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-design-anthropic-labs) or [Stitch](../../5_Library/prototyping/tools/stitch.md) generates it from a short brief; you review, adjust a token or two, and pass it to your builder so every screen looks like one product.

- **Status:** Tool-generated — Stage 01 (front end).
- **Template:** [templates/DESIGN.MD](templates/DESIGN.MD)
- **How:** follow "How to generate this" in the template, then paste the tool's output into the file.

### DATA.MD — Database Schema

Your tables, fields, and access rules. Gives the AI a precise target instead of letting it guess your data model. You only need it once your build remembers things — the backend stage of the cycle. Skip it for a frontend-only prototype.

- **Status:** Optional — Stage 03 (backend).
- **Template:** [templates/DATA.MD](templates/DATA.MD)
- **How:** paste prompt 1 to extract your tables from the PRD, then prompt 2 to fill each table's fields.

### API.MD — API Design Document

The endpoints your backend exposes, the data in and out, and how errors are handled. Reach for it once you are in a real coding agent with a backend beyond basic create-read-update-delete — the production-grade stage of the cycle.

- **Status:** Optional — Stage 04 (production-grade).
- **Template:** [templates/API.MD](templates/API.MD)
- **How:** paste your PRD into prompt 1 to draft the endpoint list, then prompts 2–5 for contracts, auth, and error conventions.

## Part 2 — The development cycle

Here is the move most founders miss: **you do not pick one tool and commit.** These four stages are one continuous path — the same product growing from a look, to a working app, to real saved data, to production-grade code. Walk as far as your hypothesis needs, then stop. Two of the handoffs are smooth and one is manual — we tell you which, so nothing surprises you.

```
01 Front end  →  02 Full web app  →  03 Backend  →  04 Production-grade
Stitch ·         Google AI Studio     Supabase ·     Claude Code ·
Claude Design                         Firebase       Codex
```

> **The full toolbox.** Each stage below names the tools we recommend — but they are not the only options. The [**Prototyping Guides** library](https://app.notion.com/p/wncp-ai/37dc814a1f2880db895eff15940127b2?v=37dc814a1f2880a7983c000cfbec2989) in Notion has the complete set: every builder, database, and agent, researched and tagged by level, type, and free tier.

### Stage 01 — Front end · make it look real

**Tools:** [Stitch](../../5_Library/prototyping/tools/stitch.md), [Claude Design](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-design-anthropic-labs) · **Specs:** UI.MD, DESIGN.MD

**Goal:** A good-looking, clickable version of your key screens. No real data yet — just the look and the flow a user walks.

1. **Generate your screens.** Open [Stitch](https://stitch.withgoogle.com) (sign in with Google). Paste your PRD's core flow and your UI.MD screen list and let it generate the screens. Stitch is multimodal — you can also drop in a reference image to steer the look.
2. **Make it yours, not generic.** Use [Claude Design](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-design-anthropic-labs) (paid tiers) — or plain Claude Artifacts on the free tier — to produce your DESIGN.MD: colors, type, spacing, components. Feed that back into Stitch so every screen is one consistent product.
3. **Iterate and click through.** Refine in the tool until the whole flow feels right end to end.
4. **Get the code out.** Copy Stitch's generated front-end code, or paste the design into Figma if a designer will refine it first. You carry this into Stage 2.

**Done when** you can click through your main flow and it looks like a real product.
**Stop here → the Frontend-only version** — a clickable demo or landing page to test interest.

### Stage 02 — Full web app · make it work

**Tool:** [Google AI Studio](../../5_Library/prototyping/tools/google-ai-studio.md) (Build mode) · **Specs:** PRD.MD, TECH_STACK.MD

**Goal:** Turn the static front end into a working web app — forms that submit, buttons that act, logic that runs.

1. **Open Build mode.** Go to [aistudio.google.com/apps](https://aistudio.google.com/apps) → Build. It scaffolds a full-stack app for you — a React front end and a Node server — with your Gemini API key wired in server-side automatically.
2. **Bring your front end in — this seam is manual.** There is no one-click import from Stitch. Either paste your Stitch code into the build prompt ("here is my UI, build the app around it"), or screenshot your screens and drop the images in — Build mode reads images as design reference. Give it your PRD.MD and TECH_STACK.MD as context.
3. **Wire up the behavior.** Describe the interactions in plain language. Preview the app live in the browser and keep prompting until the core flow runs.
4. **Ship a shareable URL.** Use Build mode's one-click deploy to Google Cloud Run — pick your Google Cloud project and you get a public link. Previewing is free; a deployed app and paid Gemini models can incur usage cost.

**Done when** a real person can open the URL and complete your core flow.
**Stop here → the Full web app version** — a working app that needs no accounts or saved data.

### Stage 03 — Backend · make it remember

**Tools:** [Supabase](https://supabase.com), or AI Studio's built-in [Firebase](https://firebase.google.com) · **Specs:** DATA.MD

**Goal:** Add accounts and data that persists — between visits, and across different users.

1. **Write DATA.MD first.** List your tables and fields. This is the precise target your builder needs — without it, the AI guesses your data model and you pay for it later.
2. **Pick a backend.**
   - **Built-in (fastest).** Staying in AI Studio, Build mode auto-provisions a Firebase backend — a Firestore database plus Firebase Authentication — with zero setup. Best when your data is simple and you want no config.
   - **Supabase (portable).** A real Postgres database with auth, file storage, and realtime in one place. Best when you want SQL and a backend you can keep and grow — especially if you will take the app into Claude Code. Create a free project at [supabase.com](https://supabase.com), grab your keys, and have your builder connect to it.
3. **Wire it up.** Prompt your builder to create the tables from DATA.MD, add sign-up and log-in, and save and load real records.
4. **Test with two accounts.** Confirm each account sees only its own data and that it survives a sign-out and a refresh.

> **Guardrail:** even with a real backend, store no permanently-stored sensitive PII and handle no real payments.

**Done when** you sign out, come back, and your data is still there.
**Stop here → the App + backend version** — real accounts and real saved data.

### Stage 04 — Production-grade · own the code

**Tools:** [Claude Code](../../5_Library/prototyping/tools/claude-code.md), [OpenAI Codex](../../5_Library/prototyping/tools/openai-codex.md) · **Specs:** all six

**Goal:** Move the whole codebase into a real coding agent to refine, extend, fix, and prepare for real users. This is the bridge to engineering-grade.

1. **Get the code into GitHub.** From AI Studio's Build mode, push your app to a GitHub repo (or download the ZIP and push it). This is the cleanest handoff in the whole pipeline — a standard repo, not a proprietary export.
2. **Open it in Claude Code.** Clone the repo, `cd` into it, and run `claude`. It reads your entire codebase at once — front end and server. Codex works the same way as an alternative.
3. **Hand it your spec set.** Point it at PRD.MD, TECH_STACK.MD, UI.MD, DESIGN.MD, DATA.MD, and API.MD. Now the agent has the same context you do.
4. **Build in plain language.** "Add X." "Fix the bug where Y." "Make this faster." It edits across files, runs the app, writes tests, and ships commits and pull requests.
5. **Deploy on real rails.** Connect the GitHub repo to [Vercel](https://vercel.com) — every push to your main branch deploys automatically. Keep the database on [Supabase](https://supabase.com) or [Neon](https://neon.tech).

**Done when** you have a repo you — or a developer — can keep building on.
**This is the Full-stack version** — and the on-ramp to a real production app.

## Part 3 — Where to stop

The cycle is one path, but you do not have to walk all of it. These are the four places founders most often stop — pick the one your hypothesis actually needs. Going further than that is not progress; it is delay. The fastest credible test wins.

| Version | Stops after | You get | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Frontend-only** | Stage 01 | A clickable, good-looking UI on a shareable URL. No backend. | Landing pages, clickable demos, testing whether anyone wants it. |
| **Full web app** | Stage 02 | A working app a real person can use — no accounts, nothing saved. | Single-user tools, testing whether the core flow actually works. |
| **App + backend** | Stage 03 | Real accounts and data that persists across visits and users. | Multi-user products, testing retention and real usage. |
| **Full-stack** | All four stages | A real codebase you own, in a repo, on production rails. | Technical founders, or anyone with a developer, heading for production. |

### Going mobile — a branch off the cycle

The cycle above is web-first — and for a prototype that is almost always the right call. A web app reaches every phone with no install and no app store. When your PRD genuinely needs a phone experience, you have two realistic paths inside a session:

- **Path A — PWA (recommended).** Any web app from the cycle installs to a phone. Open the URL on a phone and tap "Add to Home Screen" — it gets an icon and launches full-screen. Android support is first-class; iOS works with a few limits (Add-to-Home-Screen works; web push needs iOS 16.4+). Fastest thing you can hand a user to test today.
- **Path B — Expo Go preview.** For a native-feeling preview, build a React Native app in [Bolt](../../5_Library/prototyping/tools/bolt.md) with the Expo template and preview it on a real phone via the free [Expo Go](https://expo.dev/go) app — scan a QR code and it runs on your device.

App Store and Google Play submission is out of scope for a prototype session — it needs paid developer accounts, signing, a native build pipeline, and review that takes days. Build the PWA first, test with real users, then decide whether a native build is worth it.

## Capture

When your prototype is working — meaning a real person could use it and give you a yes or no on your hypothesis — do two things before the session ends.

**Save it.** Put the prototype in your `4_Dev_Projects/` project folder. If it is a live URL, create a file called `prototype-day1.md` and paste the URL and a one-line description into it. If it is a file or deck, save it directly. Day 3 and Peer Court locate the baseline using this folder and filename.

**Write the hypothesis.** Complete this sentence and save it in the same file:

> This prototype tests [what you built]. A pass = [what you observe when a user succeeds].

This sentence is your contract with Day 3 and Peer Court. It is a falsifiable test — not a tagline, not a vision statement. If you cannot complete it, your scope is not tight enough yet: go back to Section 5 of your PRD.MD and tighten the one flow before you build.
