**WHY THIS MATTERS**

**Most founders skip this. That is why most products fail.**

Every product is built on assumptions. Assumptions about who has the problem, how painful it is, what they do today to solve it, and whether they will pay to fix it. The founders who get this right do not build more. They build less, but they build the right thing.

|  | *"Most founders go into customer interviews hoping to confirm what they already believe. That is not discovery. It is pitching with extra steps."* |
| :---- | :---- |

Customer discovery is the discipline of testing your assumptions before they become architecture decisions, hiring decisions, and fundraising narratives built on the wrong foundation. The two tools in this kit handle the entire process, from hypothesis to interview to pattern.

**HOW TO USE**

**The sequence that makes this work.**

| 1 | Install the Customer Discovery Coach skill 1. In your downloaded folder, find `customer-discovery-coach.skill` inside `1-Customer-Discovery/`. 2. In Claude, go to **Customize → Skills**, click **+**, then **Upload a skill**, and select that file. 3. Toggle the skill on. The coach is now available in any Claude session — Claude will use it automatically when you ask about customer interviews or hypotheses, or you can prompt it directly. The coach will ask whether you have hypotheses already or are starting from scratch — either way works. By the end you will have a ranked hypothesis stack and a full interview guide. |
| :---: | :---- |

| 2 | Paste your hypotheses into the Guide tab Open the tracker. Enter your hypotheses in the Guide tab — this is the only place you will type them. Every other tab pulls from here automatically. |
| :---: | :---- |

| 3 | Paste your interview questions into the Question Log Copy the questions from your skill session into the Question Log, grouped by interview stage. Tag each question with the hypothesis number it is testing. |
| :---: | :---- |

| 4 | Run your interviews — ask the same questions to everyone The Question Log is built for consistency. Every interviewee gets the same questions. Log their key quotes in the cells as you go, or immediately after. |
| :---: | :---- |

| 5 | Update the Hypothesis Matrix after each interview Mark each hypothesis Validated, Invalidated, or Inconclusive for that interviewee. Be honest. An Invalidated hypothesis is more valuable than a dishonest Inconclusive. |
| :---: | :---- |

| 6 | Review the Pattern Summary after every 5 interviews The summary auto-calculates. Add your pattern notes and look for segment divergence — where small businesses and enterprises (or any two groups) see the problem differently. That is where your most important insight usually lives. |
| :---: | :---- |
